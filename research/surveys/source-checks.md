# Survey source checks

Checked November 7, 2026. These checks correct the research inventory; they are not a completed review of all 19 candidates.

| Issue | Evidence | Treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CSET snapshot could be counted as an independent sample | The [2022 snapshot](https://cset.georgetown.edu/publication/dod-and-the-u-s-tech-sector-relationship/) explicitly compares earlier CSET and RAND surveys | Link it to those underlying studies; do not add its respondents again |
| CSET sample size, fieldwork, and response rate disagree across publications | The snapshot describes a 2019 survey with 164 responses and a 1.4% response rate. The [original report](https://cset.georgetown.edu/wp-content/uploads/CSET-Cool-Projects-or-Expanding-the-Efficiency-of-the-Murderous-American-War-Machine.pdf), methods section C, p. 42, describes 2020 distributions, 160 responses, and a four-percent response rate | Preserve the discrepancy. Use the original report's own methods when describing its results; do not silently reconcile the figures |
| Anthropic fieldwork dates differ between samples | The [appendix](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/1bc1a3067f9c9883d6d556b38f26fd4f1e1b640f/The%20AI%20Expectations%20Gap%20-%20Appendix.pdf), section A, gives August 11–23, 2026 for the public sample and August 9–12 for staff | Do not assign the staff dates to the public sample |
| Anthropic completed interviews are not unique respondents | The same appendix reports 16,395 interviews reduced to 10,980 unique public respondents, plus 1,334 staff respondents | Use the appropriate respondent count and each item's actual denominator |
| Anthropic groups have different response designs | The appendix documents public “not sure” answers excluded by item and staff skipping. The [article](https://www.anthropic.com/research/ai-expectations-gap) describes sliders for staff versus ranges for some public questions | Comparisons need instrument and missing-response checks; a group difference alone cannot identify an employment effect |
| ESPAI headline sample size is larger than many item samples | [Official methods](https://wiki.aiimpacts.org/ai_timelines/predictions_of_human-level_ai_timelines/ai_timeline_surveys/2023_expert_survey_on_progress_in_ai) document randomized wording and subsets | Reconstruct exposure and response counts for each analysis |
| National-security practitioners are a different target population | [IST's report page](https://securityandtechnology.org/virtual-library/report/ai-and-the-future-of-national-security/) identifies 111 national-security professionals and experts | Keep its results separate from estimates about tech workers generally |
| Morning Consult defense-work figures lack a verified primary methods check here | The candidate links to [Morning Consult](https://intel.morningconsult.com/mc-content/analysis/tech-layoffs-defense-survey-data) and [Axios coverage](https://www.axios.com/2023/03/17/tech-workers-military-defense-work-poll); the primary methods were not accessible during the earlier check | Keep as an unverified lead; do not repeat its sample size or percentages as checked findings |

Other reading-list cautions remain to be checked against the full texts: the Epoch and Field/Douglas/Krueger entries are described as interviews; LEAP reports forecasts; the UCL report and follow-up may reuse one fielding; and AAAI panel recommendations must not be mistaken for answers to survey questions. These are screening notes, not verified findings for those papers.
